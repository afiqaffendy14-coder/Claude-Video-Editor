# The £1 Team That Won Everything? 💷

Broad-audience short, exactly 60 s, continuous voiceover, looped ending (the last line runs straight into the first).

## Script v1 (awaiting approval before voiceover)

Story shape: impossible-bargain question -> the team is abandoned -> one man refuses -> the £1 deal -> the
underdog car with a secret -> shock first race -> fight and vindication -> double title -> twist (it becomes the
Mercedes dynasty) -> back to the question. Transitions carried by the idea, no "then"/"next".

1. How does a team bought for one pound beat the biggest car makers on Earth?
2. December 2008: the financial crisis hits, and Honda quits Formula One overnight.
3. A whole factory is told their team may never race again.
4. But their boss, Ross Brawn, refuses to let it die.
5. He buys the entire team from Honda for a symbolic single pound.
6. Barely any sponsors, a plain white car, and a last-minute Mercedes engine.
7. Nobody expects much, but the car hides a secret underneath.
8. Its floor is shaped to suck it down onto the road, so it sticks like glue through the corners.
9. It comes from a gap in the rules that most teams missed.
10. In the first race, the two white cars finish first and second.
11. Jenson Button wins six of the first seven races.
12. Rivals protest, judges rule it legal, and the underdogs keep winning.
13. By the end of the year, the one-pound team wins both world titles.
14. Within weeks, Mercedes buys most of the team.
15. That factory becomes Mercedes, winning eight team titles in a row.
16. Maybe the greatest bargain in sport, and it all started with one question...

(loops to line 1: "...it all started with one question... How does a team bought for one pound beat the biggest car makers on Earth?")

## Facts and sources

- Honda announced on 5 December 2008 that it was leaving F1 with immediate effect, because of the financial crisis;
  the factory's jobs were in the balance.
  ([ESPN](https://africa.espn.com/f1/story/_/id/28167282/the-untold-stories-brawn-gp-f1-fairy-tale-nearly-never-happened),
  [F1 Wiki 2009 season](https://f1.fandom.com/wiki/2009_Formula_One_Season))
- Ross Brawn completed a 100% buyout for a symbolic £1 in March 2009 and renamed it Brawn GP; Mercedes engines.
  ([Wikipedia: Brawn GP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brawn_GP), [Sky Sports](https://www.skysports.com/f1/news/5012657/brawn-buys-out-honda))
- Australia 2009: Button won lights-to-flag, Barrichello second (a Brawn 1-2). Button won six of the first seven races.
  Both titles were secured at the 2009 Brazilian GP. ([Wikipedia: 2009 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Formula_One_World_Championship))
- Double diffuser (lines 7-9, explained without the jargon): the diffuser is the shaped underside at the back that
  sucks air from under the car, pulling it onto the road for more grip. Brawn read the 2009 rules to allow a
  double-deck version; only Brawn, Toyota and Williams started the season with one ("most teams missed").
  Protested by rivals and ruled legal by the FIA Court of Appeal in April 2009.
- Engine: the car was designed around a Honda engine; the Mercedes engine deal came late, weeks before the season
  ("last-minute").
- Mercedes: on 16 November 2009 Mercedes-Benz (with Aabar Investments) bought 75.1%, hence "most of the team";
  renamed Mercedes GP for 2010; Brawn and Nick Fry sold the rest in 2011.
  ([Crash.net](https://www.crash.net/f1/news/167211/1/brawn-100m-better-off-after-merc-sale))
- The Brackley factory team won eight constructors' titles in a row, 2014-2021.
- Caveats: "a symbolic single pound" (the price was nominal, and the deal carried other terms); "arguably" the
  greatest bargain is opinion, phrased as such. Not verified, so left out: the often-quoted ~700 jobs figure and
  Button's pay cut.
