# The Pit Stop That Made a Children's Hospital Safer

Broad-audience short, 56–60 s, continuous voiceover, looped ending (the last line runs straight into the first).
About 160 words, the same density as the Lauda v3 script (58 s).

## Script

1. Ferrari's pit crew helped make a children's hospital safer.
2. Great Ormond Street, London.
3. A baby has just survived heart surgery.
4. But one of the riskiest moments is still ahead.
5. The move from the operating theatre to intensive care.
6. Breathing tube, drips, monitors, all unplugged, moved, plugged back in.
7. Several teams crowd one tiny patient. Details get lost.
8. Then two of its doctors studied a Ferrari pit stop.
9. Around twenty mechanics. One car. Done in seconds.
10. Nobody in anyone's way. Everyone with one job.
11. So they asked Ferrari for help.
12. Ferrari's crew studied videos of the hospital's handovers.
13. The fix came straight from the pit lane. One leader, like the lollipop man, runs everything.
14. Every person gets one task, in a fixed order.
15. A checklist, so nothing is forgotten.
16. Technical errors fell by forty-two percent.
17. Missing information, almost cut in half.
18. A sport obsessed with tenths of a second taught doctors to protect what matters most.
19. That's the story of how...

(loops to line 1)

## Facts and sources

- Doctors: heart surgeon Martin Elliott and intensive care head Allan Goldman, Great Ormond Street Hospital (GOSH), London.
- They visited Ferrari in Maranello; Ferrari's team analysed the hospital's handovers.
- The anaesthetist became the single leader of the handover, modelled on the lollipop man; the protocol also drew on aviation.
  Principles: leadership, task allocation, rhythm, standardised process, checklists, communication.
- Results (WSJ / Catchpole et al., *Paediatric Anaesthesia* 2007, about 50 handovers): technical errors per handover
  fell 42% (5.42 to 3.15), information omissions fell 49%.
- Caveat: the study did not measure deaths, so the script says "safer", never "saved lives".
- Not verified, so left out: the story that they first saw the pit stop on TV after a long operation.
