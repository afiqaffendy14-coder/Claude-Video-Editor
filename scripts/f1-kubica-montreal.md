# Same Wall, One Year Later (Robert Kubica, Montreal 2007 → 2008)

Broad-audience short, about 57 s, continuous voiceover. **No loop:** the story ends on its payoff and cuts.
Status: draft, awaiting the owner's approval before voiceover.

## 1. Video concept

- **Title:** He Hit This Wall at Almost 300 km/h. A Year Later, He Came Back.
- **Core story:** At the 2007 Canadian Grand Prix, Robert Kubica touched another car, was launched by a bump in the
  grass and hit a concrete wall. The car tumbled across the track and broke apart. He had only a concussion and a
  sprained ankle. One year later, at the same track, the race leader (Lewis Hamilton) crashed into Kimi Räikkönen at a
  red light in the pit lane, and Kubica took his first and only F1 win, which put him top of the championship.
- **Target audience:** non-fans and casual viewers first; F1 fans get the Hamilton pit-lane twist.
- **Main curiosity angle:** "What happens when a driver goes back to the place that nearly destroyed his car with him
  inside?"

## 2. Script v1 (initial)

About 58 s, about 180 words.

> Robert Kubica's car hit a wall so hard, it broke into pieces.
>
> Montreal, 2007. At almost three hundred kilometres per hour, Kubica clips another car. A bump in the grass throws
> the car's nose into the air. He can't steer. He can't brake.
>
> The car hits a concrete wall, flips, and tumbles across the track. Kubica said later he knew he was in big trouble.
> His body reportedly took seventy-five times the force of gravity.
>
> Doctors found a concussion and a sprained ankle. Nothing else.
>
> His team rested him for one race. His replacement was a nineteen-year-old called Sebastian Vettel, who later won
> four world titles.
>
> June 2008. Kubica returns to Montreal. Lewis Hamilton is leading when the front-runners all pull into the pits
> together. At the exit, a red light tells them to wait. Kubica stops. Kimi Räikkönen stops. Hamilton doesn't see it
> in time. He slams into the back of Räikkönen. Both are out.
>
> Kubica races to the front and wins. His first Formula One victory, at the track where he hit that wall.

## 3. Retention analysis (prediction, not data)

| Section | Potential problem | Why viewers might leave |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (0–3 s) | Strong image, but only one question: "did he survive?" | Nothing tells viewers the story goes past the crash. |
| Crash (3–18 s) | Good escalation; "75 times gravity" is abstract | Fine. It's short, and it follows the most dramatic line. |
| "Nothing else." (~20 s) | **The only open question is answered here** | Once viewers know he's fine, the tension is gone. With no new question set up, this is the natural moment to swipe. |
| Vettel line (~22–28 s) | A detour into a different driver | Non-fans don't know Vettel; fans already know this. It stalls the story right where it is weakest. |
| Pit-lane twist (30–48 s) | Needs simple wording | Fine. "Red light, he doesn't stop" is easy to follow. |
| Ending | Stops at the win | Good, but it leaves out the biggest stake (the championship lead). |

**Biggest retention risk:** 0:18–0:28. The hook only asks "did he survive?", so the story closes its own loop at
"Nothing else." Then the Vettel detour adds no new tension. Viewers have no reason to wait for 2008.

**Fix:** put the open loop in the hook ("One year later, he went back to the same track"), so the survival beat
becomes a step on the way and not the ending. Cut the Vettel detour. Add "out of hospital the next day" to keep the
shock moving. End on the championship lead, which is the strongest final fact.

## Fact sheet (for the description)

- 2007 Canadian GP, lap 27: Kubica touched Jarno Trulli's Toyota before the hairpin. A hump in the grass lifted the
  nose, and he hit the wall. Reported speeds vary from 280 to 300 km/h, so the script says "almost 300".
- The 75 G peak is a *reported* figure (no primary measurement found), so the script says "reportedly".
- Injuries: a light concussion and a sprained right ankle. He was released from hospital the next day. He missed the
  US GP as a precaution (Vettel's debut) and returned at the French GP.
- "Knew he was in big trouble": Kubica, looking back on the crash (GPblog).
- 2008 Canadian GP (8 June): Hamilton, the leader, hit Räikkönen at the red light at the pit exit, and both retired.
  Kubica won ahead of Heidfeld (a BMW Sauber one-two). It was his first F1 win, and it took him to the top of the
  championship.

Sources: RacingNews365, GPblog, Motor Authority, AFP (afpbb), Autosport, Formula1.com, The Race, Wikipedia (2007 and
2008 Canadian GP).
