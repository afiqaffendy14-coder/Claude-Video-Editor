# Could an F1 Car Drive Upside Down? 🙃

Broad-audience short, exactly 60 s, continuous voiceover, looped ending (the last line runs straight into the first).

## Script (approved by the owner, 2026-11-02)

Story shape: wild question -> the secret is a plane wing flipped over -> the numbers line up -> the dream shot
(the car on the ceiling of the Monaco tunnel) -> the dream falls apart -> honest verdict -> back to the question.
Transitions carried by the idea, no "then"/"next".

1. Could a Formula One car drive on the ceiling?
2. It sounds impossible. The secret is in the wings.
3. A plane's wing lifts it into the sky.
4. Flip that wing over, and the air pushes the car down instead.
5. Engineers call it downforce, and the faster you go, the harder it pushes.
6. An F1 car, driver included, weighs about eight hundred kilos.
7. At around two hundred kilometres an hour, by some estimates, the air presses down just as hard.
8. Any faster, and the air beats gravity.
9. So in theory, flip the world upside down, and the car would stick.
10. The perfect place to try? Monaco's tunnel, where cars already blast through at over two hundred and fifty.
11. Picture it sweeping up the wall and onto the roof.
12. But here, the dream falls apart.
13. Engines are built for gravity pulling one way; flip them, and fuel and oil slosh into the wrong places.
14. Lose speed for even a moment, and the car drops like a stone.
15. So far, no F1 car has done it. The physics says yes; the engineering says not yet.
16. And that leaves one question hanging...

(loops to line 1: "...one question hanging... Could a Formula One car drive on the ceiling?")

## Facts and sources

- Principle: F1 wings are inverted aerofoils; downforce grows with speed (roughly with speed squared).
- Mass: the 2022-2025 minimum car mass including the driver is 798 kg ("about eight hundred kilos").
- Threshold speed (theory only, estimates vary): a University of Leicester physics paper puts the minimum at
  58.7 m/s (211 km/h) and notes the Monaco tunnel's top speed is about 260 km/h
  ([Leicester, "Ceiling It"](https://journals.le.ac.uk/index.php/pst/article/view/5066)); Driver61/The Drive say
  roughly 130 mph (~209 km/h) ([Driver61](https://driver61.com/formula-one/project-inversion-driving-f1-car-upside-down/),
  [The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/news/how-a-youtuber-plans-to-drive-an-f1-style-car-upside-down-for-real)).
  Hence "by some estimates, at around two hundred".
- Engines: Driver61's attempt plans an electric motor because a combustion engine would be too dangerous to run
  upside down; running the engine inverted was an open problem.
  ([Sports Gazette](https://sportsgazette.co.uk/project-inversion-a-youtuber-driver61s-plan-to-drive-a-race-car-upside-down/))
- Tunnel shape and transitions also cost downforce ([PlanetF1](https://www.planetf1.com/features/revealed-can-f1-car-drive-upside-down)).
- Caveat: no real F1 car has done it; Driver61's project uses a hill-climb car and was not reported complete. Other
  non-F1 cars (fan cars) are a separate story, so the script only says "no Formula One car".
