# Why Do F1 Drivers Have Huge Necks? 💪

Broad-audience short, exactly 60 s, continuous voiceover, looped ending (the last line runs straight into the first).

## Script (approved by the owner, 2026-11-02)

Story shape: body question -> put the viewer in the seat ("your head") -> the force builds -> the shock image
(a ten-year-old hanging off your helmet) -> it never stops -> what's at stake -> the strange training -> back to
the question. Second person throughout so the viewer feels it. Transitions carried by the idea, no "then"/"next".

1. Why do Formula One drivers have necks like bodybuilders?
2. Your head, plus a racing helmet, weighs about seven kilos.
3. Now take a corner at close to three hundred kilometres an hour.
4. The car turns, but your head wants to keep going straight.
5. In the fastest corners, drivers feel over five g: five times gravity.
6. That seven-kilo head suddenly weighs about thirty-five.
7. Like a ten-year-old hanging off the side of your helmet.
8. The world's most extreme rollercoaster only matches that for a moment.
9. Drivers take it corner after corner, lap after lap, for up to two hours.
10. Every time they brake, the head is flung forward too.
11. If the neck gives up, the head tips, the eyes lose the corner, and the lap is gone.
12. So drivers train their necks almost every day.
13. Straps pull their heads from every direction while they fight to hold still.
14. Some lie on a bench and lift weights with just their heads.
15. A muscle most of us never think about can decide who's fastest at the end of the race.
16. So now you know what to say when someone asks...

(loops to line 1: "...when someone asks... Why do Formula One drivers have necks like bodybuilders?")

## Facts and sources

- Cornering loads: over 5 g at the apex of the fastest corners, 4-6 g typical in fast corners; braking about 5 g.
  ([Mercedes-AMG F1: G-force explained](https://mercedesamgf1.com/news/g-force-and-formula-one-explained),
  [GPFans](https://www.gpfans.com/en/f1-news/1010709/f1-g-force), [Flow Racers](https://flowracers.com/blog/f1-g-forces/))
- Head + helmet about 7 kg; 5 g x 7 kg = 35 kg (a 6.5 g corner works out at about 45 kg). A ten-year-old weighs
  about 30-35 kg. ([GPFans](https://www.gpfans.com/en/f1-news/99919/f1-drivers-danger-neck-muscles-whiplash),
  [BJSM blog](https://blogs.bmj.com/bjsm/2013/03/26/the-fast-and-the-furiousand-physiotherapy-training-for-extreme-g-force-loads-on-the-neck))
- Rollercoaster: F1.com noted Hamilton's 6.5 g peak was 0.2 g more than "the most extreme rollercoaster on the
  planet", and coasters give only brief spikes.
  ([Formula1.com](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/video-analysing-2017s-massive-rises-in-g-force.vX8IhGjqmsaCoyy2uKKOi))
- Races are capped at two hours of running ("up to two hours").
- Training: resistance straps/harness pulling from the side, front and back; weight plates for the head; Lando
  Norris reportedly trains his neck at least five times a week ("almost every day").
  ([Oversteer48](https://oversteer48.com/norris-neck-training/), [Jalopnik](https://www.jalopnik.com/2224084/how-f1-driver-train-to-handle-g-forces/))
- Caveat: the 7 kg / 35 kg numbers are rounded estimates; the script says "about".
